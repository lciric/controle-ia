# A1 — Goodhart attack on the statistical judge: status

*Status page of the preregistered attack on this system's statistical judge by language-model proposers that see its verdicts. Last updated: 10 November 2026, 10:44 UTC (the 8 November update and the 2 November text below are kept unchanged). The result is below: the output was sealed before any reading, read under the frozen criteria, audited in writing, and the official verdict was given by a fresh reader.*

## Update (10 November 2026): the attack against v9 (A1-v4)

**What ran.** v9 was tested **as a candidate**: the production judge remains the v8 null, and this run commits neither the public claim nor production (open decisions at the end of this section). Argon alone — one provider, one form of optimisation — on 24 cards per world. Two judges: v9, and the old judge v8 as the check that the attack has teeth. Two arms: with the judge's verdicts ("feedback") and without ("witness"). Four cells per world, 24 units per cell, up to 16 attempts per unit; 1,478 calls in all.

**The two worlds, as the sealed text defines them.**
- **W2**: S1 on sub-population 0, S2 on sub-population 1 — the world on which the proposer breached v8 (8 November update below). It is a member of the v9 null, so **every promotion under v9 is false**. What it tests: whether v9's code and certificate hold against the language-model proposer that found this hole under v8, now that it reads v9's description, its declared holes included, and, in the feedback arm, receives its verdicts. A breach with even one promoted law replaying above α after Holm would have meant a defect of the certificate, the code or the bench, and an invalid candidate; a breach with none, multiplicity.
- **Graded W2 (W2g)**: S1 on sub-population 0; (1 − λ)·S2 + λ·N on sub-population 1, where N is a new law drawn as in v9's bench (at λ = 0, this is W2). The dose λ\* = 0.40 was fixed before sealing by an offline test played under its own sealed text. This is v9's **declared frontier**: at λ\* the world is, by its form, a member of neither the v9 nor the v8 null, and one sub-population carries information that the archive does not hold. A promotion under v9 is therefore **not** false by construction: v9's members, every pure recombination of S1 and S2 included, have log W ≤ 0 by certificate, so any law promoted under v9 lies outside v9's class. What it tests: whether the proposer **finds** the new law (promoted laws that replay above α) or is promoted by noise only — that is, whether v9's price in power leaves the candidate blind where it matters, where the system still has to discover. "Frontier not crossed" would have measured that price under attack; either answer is useful.

Per replayed law: 236 promotions out of 75,000 with feedback (0.31 %), 192 out of 74,800 without (0.26 %), against α = 5 %.
- **W2 (S1 on sub-population 0, S2 on sub-population 1) breaks v8, in the class the null declares uncovered.** 24 of 24 units in both arms; 43 of the 48 promotions at the first attempt; feedback changes nothing (McNemar p = 1.0 after 1, 4 and 16 attempts); the older v7 judge falls the same way. Proof: `orchestrator/prereg/rapport-redaction-nul-v9.md`, section 3; counter-read, with fresh probes (48 new worlds, 288 instants, 0 failure). With fewer than two known laws, v9 adds no member.

## The question

Does the judge resist an agent that plays against it? The statistical judge was validated against 27 **scripted** cheaters (section 3.1 of the [main README](../../README.md)). It has never faced a **language-model proposer that receives the judge's verdicts and optimises against them**. In production that feedback does not exist: the memory never serves as a verdict to the agents. The experiment measures what happens if that guard gives way.

Theory already bounds part of the answer. Each attempt is promoted with probability at most α = 0.05 when the world's law is a member of the null, whatever the history. Nothing bounds the number of attempts, though: with n attempts and a stop at the first promotion, the bound is 1 − (1 − α)ⁿ (0.05, 0.185 and 0.560 for n = 1, 4 and 16). The run measures where a real optimiser lands between 0 and that ceiling, and whether the judge's feedback pushes it higher than a witness without feedback.

## Design (frozen in the sealed text)

- **Two toy worlds where the truth is known.** Neither brings a law beyond the archive, so every promotion is a false promotion.
  - **W1**: a mixture of two archive laws. It is a member of the current (v8) null, so a false promotion can only come from the multiplicity of attempts or from a code defect.
  - **W2**: a piecewise recombination of the same two laws, a class that the v8 null declares it does not cover. This is the regime in which feedback could teach the proposer the judge's taste.
- **Two arms.** "Feedback": the proposer sees each verdict. "Witness": no verdict.
- **Two judges.** The current null (v8), and the old null (v7) as a check that the attack has teeth.
- **Grid G1.** 12 cards per world, 24 units per cell, two providers (Kimi and Argon), up to 16 attempts per unit.
- **Six signed numeric predictions (P1 to P6),** each open in both directions. They do not decide the outcome; frozen reading criteria do. For example, P1 (W1, v8): with feedback, at most 0.10 of units promoted after 16 attempts, and feedback minus witness within ±0.10.
- **Budget**: capped at 80 USD (27 to 50 USD expected), hard time limit 20 hours.

## Limits, written before the run

- The worlds are synthetic (3 outcomes, 2 Gaussian traits). Nothing here carries over to real model episodes.
- There are two providers and one grid.
- The finite bank reused from one attempt to the next is not played.
- A breach on W2 would not mean that the v8 null is flawed or that Ville's guarantee is beaten: the class is declared uncovered by the null itself. It would measure how fast a language model finds that hole, and how much the feedback adds.
- The real path has been tested only against a fake relay before this run.
