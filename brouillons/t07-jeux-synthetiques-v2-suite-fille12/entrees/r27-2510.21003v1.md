## Problem statement (the only description of the task you may use)

Image autoregressive (AR) models have recently achieved state-of-the-art performance in high-fidelity image synthesis, surpassing other generative approaches such as VAEs, GANs and diffusion models. Most recent image AR models rely on vector quantization: an encoder maps an image to a lower-resolution grid of continuous embeddings, a quantizer replaces each embedding with the nearest vector of a learned codebook, and a decoder reconstructs the image from the resulting sequence of discrete tokens. The AR model is trained to output, for each position, a probability vector over the codebook entries conditioned on all previous tokens, p(q_i | q_<i), and images are generated by sampling the tokens one after another, so that the likelihood of a sequence is the product of these conditionals.

Despite their strong generation ability, a key limitation of AR models lies in this inherently sequential modeling manner: token-by-token sampling requires as many steps as there are tokens (or groups of tokens), which is often a large number and makes inference slow. Numerous methods have been proposed to reduce the number of sampling steps, but nearly all fail to achieve single-step sampling without significant performance degradation. Set prediction, in which the model predicts the distribution of a set of tokens simultaneously, reduces sampling to around ten steps, but it struggles with very few steps because the correlation between tokens within a set is completely lost, and this loss becomes increasingly harmful as the set size grows. For instance, if a dataset contains only the two samples (0,0) and (1,1), predicting both coordinates in one step yields a uniform distribution over (0,0), (1,1), (0,1) and (1,0), which is incorrect. Speculative decoding, widely used for large language models because it is training-free, drafts several tokens with a cheaper sampler and verifies them in parallel with the target model, but in image AR generation it achieves only a limited compression of sampling steps (less than 3x) because of the weak modeling capacity of the draft generator.

Distilled Decoding 1 (DD1) marked a significant breakthrough as the first method able to compress the sampling process of an image AR model to a single step. Instead of sampling the next token directly from the probability vector output by the AR model, DD1 uses flow matching in the codebook embedding space to transform a noise token into a data token, which gives a token-wise deterministic mapping from noise to data while preserving the output distribution of the original AR model. Repeating this process in the original AR order yields a complete mapping from a sequence of noise tokens to a sequence of data tokens, and a new model is then distilled to learn this mapping directly, so that the entire token sequence is generated in a single forward pass. However, the constructed mapping is inherently challenging for a model to learn: DD1 still incurs a significant performance drop relative to the original AR model in the one-step setting, and its training is relatively slow. Moreover, training a generative model to fit a predefined mapping may constrain its flexibility, whereas models such as GANs and VAEs, which do not learn explicit input-output correspondences, have shown broad applicability across downstream generation tasks. More broadly, although AR models are competitive with or superior to diffusion models in sample quality, one-step sampling for AR models remains underexplored; and while few-step sampling has been studied extensively for diffusion models, AR models follow a completely different modeling approach and generation process, so the goals and challenges of accelerating them are distinct.

This raises the central question: given a well-trained teacher AR model, kept fixed, that provides the probability of the next token conditioned on all previous tokens, can we train a one-step generator that maps a latent variable drawn from a prior distribution to a complete token sequence in a single forward pass, such that its output distribution matches that of the teacher AR model, without relying on any predefined noise-to-data mapping? Such a generator should substantially narrow the quality gap to the original AR model left by existing one-step approaches while remaining efficient to train, moving image AR models toward maximal sampling efficiency without sacrificing their superior sample quality.
