## Problem statement (the only description of the task you may use)

Accurate and dense semantic understanding of dynamic scenes is a critical capability for autonomous systems, including self-driving cars, drones, and robots. The prevalent paradigm, however, relies on conventional RGB cameras that capture information at discrete, often low, frequencies (e.g., 20 Hz). This low sampling rate creates significant "perceptual gaps", or "blind spots", between frames, during which fast-moving or abruptly appearing objects are not perceived, posing a severe risk in high-speed scenarios. Consider a pedestrian who suddenly enters a vehicle's path: at one frame time, a low-frame-rate system detects no hazard; when the pedestrian appears in the next frame, it may already be too late for the autonomous system to react. This motivates high-frame-rate perception that can produce reliable predictions at any moment in time. High-speed RGB cameras could mitigate this gap, but their prohibitive cost, high power consumption, and massive data bandwidth requirements make them impractical for scalable, real-world deployment.

Event cameras, in contrast, record brightness changes asynchronously at microsecond resolution and with high dynamic range, naturally capturing high-temporal-resolution motion while consuming far less power and bandwidth. However, an event camera is temporally dense but spatially sparse, which limits its semantic information: event-only semantic segmentation methods often struggle to produce dense, high-fidelity semantic maps. An RGB camera cooperating with an event stream therefore offers a more practical and scalable way to build high-frame-rate perception.

The problem is then to predict a dense semantic map at any arbitrary timestamp $t+\delta t$ within the perceptual gap $(t, t+\Delta t]$ between two consecutive low-frame-rate frames, given only the initial RGB frame $I_t$ and the corresponding event stream $\mathcal{E}_{t-\Delta t \to t+\delta t}$. Two constraints differentiate this setting from standard paradigms: causality (no future frame such as $I_{t+\Delta t}$ may be used) and anytime prediction (a prediction must be produced for any offset $\delta t \in (0, \Delta t]$, not just at fixed frame times). Existing paradigms fail one or both of these constraints. Video semantic segmentation methods leverage temporal coherence to improve segmentation consistency and efficiency across frames, but they are fundamentally frame-based and presuppose a dense, high-frame-rate RGB video stream as input. Video interpolation and related approaches that transfer information between two known RGB frames require the future frame and are therefore non-causal. Multi-modal RGB-event fusion methods are designed for enhancement, that is, using past events to improve the segmentation of the RGB frame at time $t$ itself; they do not predict into the blind interval that follows a single frame and are therefore not anytime-capable.

This task presents a non-trivial challenge: how can the rich, static semantic context of a single past RGB frame be effectively merged with the temporally dense, but spatially sparse and often noisy, event stream, so that dense predictions remain accurate at an arbitrary target time? The difficulty is compounded in highly dynamic scenes and over long prediction intervals, where objects move, appear, or become occluded after the last frame was captured. More broadly, the question is whether reliable high-frame-rate semantic perception can be obtained from low-frame-rate RGB hardware, decoupling perceptual frequency from the sensor's frame rate.
