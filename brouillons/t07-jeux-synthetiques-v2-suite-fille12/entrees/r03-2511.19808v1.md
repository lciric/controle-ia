## Problem statement (the only description of the task you may use)

Deep neural networks have made significant strides in many domains of machine learning. These models predominantly rely on supervised learning, which depends extensively on the availability of large datasets with high-quality labels. Unfortunately, in real-world applications the quality of labels is frequently compromised by various issues, including subjective errors on samples that are hard to distinguish and objective mistakes in label recording, which makes noisy labels a common challenge. Deep neural networks are highly susceptible to overfitting when trained on data with noisy labels, and the presence of such label noise has been shown to significantly undermine their generalization ability, so that they perform poorly on clean test data. The development of robust methods for learning from noisy labels is therefore of paramount importance.

Label noise has been categorized into two primary types. Under class-conditional noise, the probability that a label is corrupted depends on the class but not on the individual instance. Under instance-dependent noise, this probability varies with instance features, so that the noise distribution correlates with feature similarities among samples; this more common type presents unique challenges and has been the focus of many recent works. Because deep neural networks tend to learn from clean data first and only subsequently adapt to noisy data, several sample selection methods use training dynamics, such as prediction loss and confidence levels, to isolate and manage noisy labels. Other methods enhance the resilience of models trained on noisy datasets through more sophisticated training regimens that incorporate label filtering, label correction (for instance, refining labels during training with the model's own predictions), reweighting of samples according to estimated noise levels or to their likelihood of being clean, robust loss formulations, and semi-supervised learning frameworks that treat suspected noisy samples as unlabeled data.

However, these methods typically lack the ability to actively explore different possibilities or to learn from the long-term consequences of their decisions, which can limit their effectiveness in more complex or highly noisy scenarios.

This leads to the following problem. Given a classification training set in which each instance is paired with an observed label that may be a corrupted, noisy version of its true label, the goal is to learn an effective prediction model. How can the noisy training labels be corrected so that the prediction model can be trained without being hindered by the label noise? In particular, can label correction be made to explore different correction possibilities and to account for the long-term consequences of its correction decisions, and can it remain effective in complex, instance-dependent and highly noisy scenarios?
