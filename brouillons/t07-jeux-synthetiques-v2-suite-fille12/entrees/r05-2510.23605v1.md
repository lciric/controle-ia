## Problem statement (the only description of the task you may use)

Current 3D and 4D (dynamic 3D) generation methods are usually optimized for photorealism, efficiency and aesthetics. They are mostly guided by text prompts, or by a single image or video that determines the front-facing appearance of the generated asset. Although this gives users a certain amount of control over the content, these methods fall short of delivering the identity-preserving outputs that personalized generation requires: they often fail to preserve the semantic identity of the specific subject across viewpoints. For example, a state-of-the-art 4D generation model given a video of a cat can render the side and back views, which were occluded in the input, with a bluish tone that the real animal does not have. Improving the personalization quality of 3D/4D generation is therefore a core challenge for enabling impact and improving user experience.

In 2D, adapting a generative model to one or a few images of a specific subject, known as personalization or subject-driven generation, has become a prominent topic: a special text token can be optimized to represent the subject, or a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model can be finetuned on a small number of example images, and such personalization has been extended to multi-subject composition and to video. Personalized 3D/4D generation, by contrast, is still largely underexplored, and the goal of personalized customization has been largely neglected as the field has focused on the geometry, consistency and efficiency of generated assets. The few existing subject-driven 3D methods use image-to-image translation with personalized 2D models on multi-view observations, use a subject-specific prior to guide score distillation, or finetune a multi-view generation model with identity-aware optimization.

Identity preservation in 3D/4D generation is challenging. When generation is guided by a single image or video, the model has few cues to infer the appearance of unobserved viewpoints and is forced to hallucinate it. Score distillation sampling optimizes the appearance of novel viewpoints under a 2D diffusion prior, but the time-consuming per-scene optimization prevents its wide use in real-world applications, and the appearance and motion of the assets often become averaged out during optimization, which can reduce quality. Multi-view diffusion models are now deployed in 3D/4D pipelines to hallucinate the appearance of a set of selected novel views, but they suffer from systematic errors in color and appearance for novel views, likely due to biases in their training data that are difficult to address without careful dataset filtering. The most recent native 3D generators, trained on massive data, directly produce a 3D representation without per-scene optimization or intermediate multi-view images and achieve superior efficiency, yet they still struggle to satisfactorily preserve the identity of the given reference.

The problem is therefore subject-driven 3D/4D generation: given a single image (for 3D) or a single video (for 4D) of a specific subject, how can one generate an asset whose appearance in every viewpoint, including the side and back views that are never observed in the input, faithfully preserves the identity of that subject while remaining consistent across views?
