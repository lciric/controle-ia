## Problem statement (the only description of the task you may use)

The world is filled with objects that vary widely in shape and material. Some are smooth and reflective, while others are rough, detailed or even translucent. Even familiar objects can appear differently from different viewpoints as light creates reflections and subtle color changes across their surfaces. Capturing this richness is important for building generative models of realistic objects. To do so, we need representations that can model both the underlying 3D geometry of real-world objects and their view-dependent appearance.

However, most existing 3D representations in machine learning tackle only part of this problem. A large body of work learns latent representations of geometry alone, aiming to recover the overall shape of objects, whether by modeling surfaces as 3D point distributions, shapes as occupancy or signed distance functions, or geometry as dense or sparse voxel grids. Grid-based latents offer structure but face inherent trade-offs between spatial resolution and memory efficiency, and methods that rely on occupancy or signed distance fields typically require significant preprocessing of the training data, such as watertight meshes, expensive mesh-to-field conversions, or optimization-based radiance-field fitting. Above all, these representations capture only geometry, without appearance, texture, or view-dependent effects.

A smaller set of recent works extends latent 3D representations to also encode appearance, but they have their own limitations. Primitive-based representations that store signed distance together with material properties such as color, roughness and metallicity require an optimization step to construct the primitives from meshes before training, making data preparation demanding. Structured latents that fuse multiview features from a foundation vision model into a sparse voxel grid require coarse occupancy to be known in advance, so generation must be performed in two stages; they average features across views, discarding angular variation and treating appearance as view-independent diffuse color; and because they rely on preconstructed axis-aligned grids, they generate objects in a canonical dataset orientation, which necessitates post-processing to align them with an input image. As a result, current models struggle to represent view-dependent effects such as reflections, highlights, or subtle changes in shading that are important for realistic appearance.

This raises two related questions. First, how can one learn a compact 3D latent representation that jointly captures an object's geometry and its view-dependent appearance, that can be trained without heavy data preprocessing, and that reproduces view-dependent effects without sacrificing geometric accuracy compared with geometry-only representations? Second, can such a representation support generation from a single image, so that the generated 3D object has a shape that matches the object as seen from the input viewpoint and an appearance that reflects the lighting and view-dependent material properties present in the input?
