## Problem statement (the only description of the task you may use)

Recent advances in vision-centric autonomous driving have driven significant progress in the field. From a representation standpoint, existing approaches can be broadly categorized into dense BEV-centric and sparse query-centric paradigms. Dense BEV-centric methods (e.g., BEVFormer and Lift-Splat-Shoot) extract Bird's Eye View (BEV) features from multi-view images for downstream tasks, while Sparse Perception Models (SPMs) bypass explicit dense BEV or volumetric representations and directly aggregate features from images using a sparse set of implicit queries. Examples include sparse 3D object detectors such as DETR3D, the PETR series, Sparse4D and SparseBEV, and query-based occupancy predictors such as OPUS, SparseOcc and OSP. Compared with BEV-based methods, these sparse models enable faster inference, reduce computational cost and broaden the perception range, and they have garnered increasing attention within the community due to their practical advantages for real-world deployment.

Despite the dominance of supervised methods, their reliance on precise ground-truth annotations presents a substantial challenge, as acquiring such labels (e.g., 3D boxes or dense 3D semantic occupancy) is both costly and labor-intensive. Conversely, the abundance of unlabeled driving data offers a promising avenue to further enhance model performance. Nevertheless, effectively leveraging this data remains a non-trivial challenge.

To exploit unlabeled data, various pre-training strategies have been proposed for autonomous driving. Earlier works leverage contrastive learning and masked autoencoders, but the coarse supervision they provide limits their capacity to fully capture spatial-temporal geometry. In contrast, NeRF-based approaches such as UniPAD and ViDAR utilize 3D volumetric differentiable rendering to reconstruct and predict 3D scene structures. To reduce memory overhead and improve rendering efficiency, a separate line of research introduces 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for explicit scene representation: GaussianPretrain predicts Gaussian parameters for feed-forward reconstruction and integrates geometric and texture representations, while VisionPAD projects neighboring frames onto the current frame using rendered depths and relative poses, relying solely on RGB image supervision rather than explicit depth annotations. These pipelines successfully model spatial-temporal representations, but they do so for dense BEV or volumetric features.

Although existing pre-training paradigms have substantially enhanced the performance of downstream applications, their reliance on dense BEV or volumetric representations limits their applicability to SPMs. Unlike dense BEV-centric perception models, the latent sparse queries in SPMs lack explicit spatial positions and semantic meanings, which makes it challenging to directly apply existing rendering-based pre-training methods. Query-based pre-training methods developed for 2D images, such as Frozen-DETR and GLID, are not a solution either, because pre-training for driving perception requires an accurate 3D geometric representation extracted from multi-view images. Transferring what is learned is a further obstacle: loading a pre-trained image backbone into a downstream SPM is straightforward, but unlike dense frameworks, which share a BEV feature map as a unified intermediate representation, SPMs lack a common structural basis. They employ task-specific queries paired with dedicated decoders whose architectures differ significantly across methods and tasks; for instance, some initialize their queries in 2D BEV space whereas others do so in 3D space. Up to now, there is no pre-training scheme that can effectively adapt to SPMs.

This raises the following research questions. How can Sparse Perception Models be pre-trained on unlabeled multi-view driving data, in a self-supervised manner, so that they learn fine-grained 3D scene representations even though they have no explicit dense intermediate representation to supervise? How can the knowledge acquired during such pre-training be transferred to heterogeneous query-based downstream models, such as sparse 3D object detectors and sparse semantic occupancy predictors, so as to improve their performance and reduce their dependence on costly annotations?
