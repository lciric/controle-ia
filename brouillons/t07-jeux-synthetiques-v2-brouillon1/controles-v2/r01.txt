TITLE: Does a norm-relative addition coefficient give layer-independent steering
PROPOSAL: We rewrite activation addition so that the coefficient is expressed as a fraction of the root-mean-square norm of the residual activation at the layer where the edit is applied, and ask whether one such fraction produces the same behavioural change at every layer, from shallow to deep, where raw coefficients do not. The study steers refusal in two open instruction-tuned families and reads, for each layer and fraction, the refusal rate on a harmful prompt set, the compliance rate on a harmless set and fluency judged on free generations, along with the raw coefficient that matches each fraction at that layer.
CONTROLS: A comparison with raw-coefficient addition at matched behavioural effect per layer and with projection removal of the refusal direction, a reference run with the unsteered model at the same decoding, the same setting repeated at every layer with the fraction held fixed, and refusal and compliance measured against a human-labelled subset of generations, over five seeds.
COST: 180
PREDICTION: A single fraction should give refusal rates within a few points across layers, while raw coefficients spread widely across the same layers. The claim is refuted if the fraction needed for a given refusal rate still changes with depth as much as the raw coefficient does.

TITLE: Steering refusal with addition coefficients scaled to each layer's activation norm
PROPOSAL: We scale the coefficient of activation addition by the root-mean-square norm of the residual activation at the edited layer, so that a single fraction describes the edit at any depth, and we ask whether this fraction moves the representation the same way across the layers of two open instruction-tuned families. For every layer and fraction we record the change in cosine between the activation and the refusal direction, the divergence between steered and unsteered next-token distributions, and the share of generations that remain fluent under a judge, on harmful and harmless prompt sets.
CONTROLS: A comparison with raw-coefficient addition at matched edit norm per layer and with projection removal of the refusal direction, a reference run with the unsteered model at the same decoding, the same setting repeated at every layer with the fraction held fixed, and the cosine shift and next-token divergence measured against the unsteered activations, over five seeds.
COST: 180
PREDICTION: A single fraction should shift the cosine by the same amount at every layer and move the next-token distribution by a steady amount. The claim is refuted if the cosine shift for a given fraction still changes with depth as much as the raw coefficient does.

TITLE: Steering through a sparse dictionary feature to leave unrelated features untouched
PROPOSAL: We replace the contrastive refusal direction by a feature learned by a sparse dictionary on the residual stream, and steer by scaling that feature's decoder vector alone, on the view that a dictionary feature overlaps less with other encoded concepts than a direction estimated from prompt pairs. We measure on two model families whether unrelated features, read through probes for topic, sentiment and language, stay in place across the full range of steering strength, together with perplexity and a general knowledge benchmark, and we record how far the chosen feature's decoder vector lies from the contrastive direction.
CONTROLS: A comparison with contrastive-direction addition at matched target effect and with projection removal along the contrastive direction, a reference run with the unsteered model at the same decoding, the same setting repeated with an unrelated dictionary feature scaled by the same amount, and probe readings for topic, sentiment and language measured against their unsteered values at every strength, over five seeds.
COST: 230
PREDICTION: Dictionary steering should move the refusal probe while the unrelated probes stay within their unsteered spread, and the unrelated feature should move its own probe only, leaving the refusal probe in place. The result counts against the method if unrelated probes shift as much as under contrastive addition at the same target effect.

TITLE: Scaling a learned dictionary feature as a refusal control in language models
PROPOSAL: We train a sparse dictionary on the residual stream of two model families, pick the feature whose activation separates refusing from complying responses best, and steer refusal by multiplying that feature's decoder vector by a strength factor, with the rest of the representation left as it is. Across the full range of the factor we track refusal rate on harmful prompts, perplexity on held-out text, a general knowledge benchmark and probes for topic, sentiment and language, and we check whether the chosen feature's rank and sparsity change with the dictionary used to find it.
CONTROLS: A comparison with the dictionary trained on a second text corpus at matched target effect and with a dictionary of twice the width, a reference run with the unsteered model at the same decoding, the same setting repeated with the dictionary refit under a different sparsity penalty, and probe readings for topic, sentiment and language measured against their values under the second-corpus dictionary at every strength, over five seeds.
COST: 230
PREDICTION: The chosen feature should move refusal smoothly with the strength factor, and its rank should stay stable across dictionaries. The result counts against the method if the feature found with the second corpus moves the probes differently from the feature found with the first.

TITLE: Where in a response graded refusal steering changes the outcome
PROPOSAL: We apply steering along the refusal direction at many strengths between removal and strong addition, and for each harmful request we locate the token at which the response commits to refusing or to helping, using the log-probability gap between refusal and compliance continuations at every position. The aim is to learn whether intermediate strengths produce mixed responses that refuse and then help, or a commitment point that slides earlier or later, on two instruction-tuned models and a harmful request collection with paired harmless rewrites.
CONTROLS: A comparison with the unsteered model sampled many times per request and with steering along a random direction of the same norm, a reference run with the harmless rewrites at every strength, the same setting repeated with the strength held fixed and the decoding temperature varied, and commitment positions measured against labels from two annotators, over five seeds.
COST: 110
PREDICTION: The commitment point should move smoothly with strength, and mixed responses should appear at intermediate strengths more often than under repeated sampling alone. Disproof is a commitment point that jumps between two positions with an empty middle, or mixed responses as frequent in unsteered samples.

TITLE: One control parameter for sycophancy and hedging across model families
PROPOSAL: We estimate directions for sycophancy, agreeing with a user's stated but wrong belief, and for hedging, adding qualifications to confident answers, from contrastive prompt pairs, and apply the same scale-free control parameter at all layers of models from three families spanning small to large. We test whether the control curve has the same shape in each family and whether the parameter value for a given effect transfers, scoring sycophancy with a factual-agreement test, hedging with a qualifier counter, and general ability with a reading comprehension benchmark at every value, and we draw every family's curve on one plot with its own scale marked.
CONTROLS: A comparison with per-layer tuned addition on the same model at matched target effect and with projection removal on the same model, a reference run with the unsteered model of the same family and scale, the same setting repeated with the directions re-estimated on a second prompt collection, and control curves measured against each other across families at the same parameter values, over five seeds.
COST: 720
PREDICTION: Curves should share their shape across families, with the parameter for a given effect within a narrow band shared by all three. The outcome refutes transfer if one family needs a parameter outside that band or shows a curve of a different shape.

PROPOSAL: From contrastive prompt pairs we derive a sycophancy direction, which captures agreement with a user's false belief, and a hedging direction, which captures added qualifications, and we steer both with one scale-free parameter applied at every layer of models from three families and several scales. TITLE: Steering refusal and an emotion at once to test non-interference
PROPOSAL: We apply two edits in the same forward pass, one along the refusal direction and one along a sadness direction, each with its own control value, on two model families, and ask whether the response on one behaviour depends on the control value of the other. If the interventions leave unrelated features alone, the refusal curve should be the same at every sadness value and the emotion curve the same at every refusal value. We score refusal with a judge on harmful prompts, emotion with a classifier on open-ended prompts, and fluency with perplexity across the whole grid of values.
CONTROLS: A comparison with each edit applied alone at the same control values and with both edits applied along a random pair of directions, a reference run with the unsteered model at the same decoding, the same setting repeated with the order of the two edits reversed, and each behaviour curve measured against its single-edit curve at every value of the other control, over five seeds.
COST: 150
PREDICTION: Each curve should overlap its single-edit curve at every value of the other control, with perplexity flat across the grid. We take as a miss any shift of the refusal curve with the sadness value larger than the spread across seeds.

TITLE: Joint control of refusal and sadness in a single forward pass
PROPOSAL: In one forward pass we add two edits to the residual stream of two model families, one along a refusal direction and one along a sadness direction, each driven by its own control value, and we chart the resulting behaviour across the full grid of the two values, asking whether each behaviour answers to its own value alone. If the control is well behaved, both behaviours should follow their own value smoothly over the grid, and generations should stay fluent at every corner of it. Refusal is scored by a judge on harmful prompts, sadness by a classifier on open-ended prompts, and fluency by perplexity, and the grid is drawn for long and short prompts and for two chat templates.
CONTROLS: A comparison with both edits applied at the same control values on short prompts and with both edits applied along a random pair of directions on a second chat template, a reference run with the unsteered model, the same setting repeated with the order of the two edits reversed, and each behaviour curve measured against its curve on the second chat template at every value of the other control, over five seeds.
COST: 150
PREDICTION: Both curves should look alike on long and short prompts and on both templates, with perplexity flat across the grid. We take as a miss any shift of the refusal curve between templates larger than the spread across seeds.
