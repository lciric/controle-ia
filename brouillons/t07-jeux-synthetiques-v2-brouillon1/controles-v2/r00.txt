TITLE: Estimating the marginal divergence with a sample classifier for diffusion fine-tuning
PROPOSAL: We regularise reward fine-tuning of a latent text-to-image diffusion model with a marginal Kullback-Leibler term estimated by a small classifier trained to separate samples of the current model from samples of the pretrained model; its logit gives the log density ratio, so the penalty touches only final images and never a trajectory. The classifier is refreshed every few updates and the policy gradient uses its ratio as a per-sample penalty on an aesthetic reward. We first run the scheme on two-dimensional mixtures where the tilted density is known in closed form, then on images with prompts drawn from a public caption collection.
CONTROLS: A comparison with trajectory-level divergence penalties and with unregularised proximal policy optimisation at matched reward queries, a reference run with the exact tilted density on the mixtures, the same setting repeated with the classifier frozen at its first fit, and the estimated divergence checked against importance-weighted estimates on held-out images, over five seeds.
COST: 520
PREDICTION: The classifier penalty should sit above the trajectory penalty on the reward-against-divergence frontier and track the exact tilted density on the mixtures. Failure shows as a frontier that falls on or under the trajectory penalty, or as a mixture fit that drifts from the closed form once the classifier is refreshed.

TITLE: Does restricting reward updates to the most sensitive noise levels improve fine-tuning
PROPOSAL: We measure, for a pretrained flow model on document layouts with a constraint-satisfaction reward, how much the final reward changes when the velocity field is perturbed at each integration time, and we fine-tune only the band of times with the highest sensitivity while leaving the rest frozen. The hypothesis is that the multi-step structure concentrates the useful signal in a few steps, so a narrow band reaches the same reward with fewer updates and less drift from the pretrained model. Reward, estimated divergence and layout validity are tracked during training, and the sensitivity profile over times is recorded for each run.
CONTROLS: A comparison with fine-tuning every integration time and with fine-tuning a band of the same width chosen at the least sensitive times, a reference run with the pretrained model under reward guidance at matched reward queries, the same setting repeated with the band width varied from one step to half the chain, and divergence measured against the pretrained model with importance weights, over five seeds.
COST: 380
PREDICTION: The sensitive band should reach the reward of full fine-tuning with less divergence, and the least sensitive band should lag far behind. A negative outcome is a sensitive band that does not separate from the least sensitive band, or full fine-tuning staying ahead at every width.

TITLE: Fine-tuning a flow model at its reward-sensitive integration times
PROPOSAL: Taking a pretrained layout flow model and a constraint-satisfaction reward, we estimate the sensitivity of the final reward to perturbations of the velocity field at every integration time, rank the times, and apply reward fine-tuning only inside the top-ranked band, keeping all other times frozen. Because sampling runs through many steps, we expect the reward signal to concentrate in a small part of the chain, so updates confined there should raise reward quickly with little departure from the pretrained model. We follow reward, importance-weighted divergence and layout validity throughout training and report the ranking of times for each run.
CONTROLS: A comparison with fine-tuning under two optimiser momentum values and with fine-tuning under a cosine decay of the step size, a reference run with the pretrained model on a second layout collection at matched reward queries, the same setting repeated with the number of elements per layout varied from four to forty, and divergence measured against the pretrained model with a second importance estimator, over five seeds.
COST: 380
PREDICTION: The top-ranked band should raise reward quickly and keep divergence small under every momentum and decay value tried. A negative outcome is a reward that climbs slowly for every optimiser choice, or divergence that grows with the number of elements per layout.

TITLE: Correcting a pretrained flow model from data samples by partial re-noising
PROPOSAL: We take a pretrained flow-matching image model whose coarse solver drifts from the data, draw real images, carry them to an intermediate time with the forward noising map, integrate the pretrained field back to the end, and fine-tune only the last part of the velocity field so that these round trips land on the original images. We run it on a face collection and on a class-conditional natural image collection, reporting Fréchet distance, precision and recall at the coarse step count throughout.

The plan fails if the corrected model lands at the same distance as the uncorrected one, or if training on the model's own samples helps as much as training on real images.

PROPOSAL: We compare iterated rejection-sampling fine-tuning, which keeps samples whose reward passes a threshold and trains on them, with policy gradient under a trajectory-level penalty, for a molecule graph diffusion model scored by a docking proxy and for a text-to-image model scored by a preference reward. We count as a miss any frontier that cannot be told apart from best-of-many selection, or an estimator that departs from the enumerable truth.

TITLE: Dense rewards from one-step denoised previews along the diffusion chain
PROPOSAL: We give the policy gradient a reward at every denoising step by scoring the one-step denoised preview of the current noisy state with the reward model, without waiting for the final image, so that credit reaches early steps directly through the chain's own structure. The per-step rewards are discounted toward the final score and combined with a trajectory penalty. We test on a latent text-to-image model with a prompt-alignment reward and on a document layout model with a constraint reward, tracking final reward, divergence from the pretrained model and the number of reward queries used, together with the share of the final gain that each quarter of the chain accounts for.
CONTROLS: A comparison with terminal-only reward at matched reward queries and with the same dense scheme where previews are scored only in the last quarter of the chain, a reference run with the pretrained model under preview-based guidance at sampling time, the same setting repeated with the discount varied from zero to one, and divergence measured against the pretrained model with importance weights, over five seeds.
COST: 640
PREDICTION: Dense previews should reach a given reward with fewer queries and less divergence than terminal-only reward, with most of the gain coming from early steps. We treat as disproof a dense run that matches terminal-only reward query for query, or a last-quarter run that keeps the whole gain by itself.
