TITLE: One reward head per annotator group so that head spread encodes disagreement
PROPOSAL: On HelpSteer2-Preference and the multi-annotator subset of PKU-SafeRLHF we cluster annotators by their voting pattern into a handful of groups, train one Bradley-Terry head per group on a shared Llama-3.1-8B-instruct backbone, and read the spread of the heads' scores on a response as its disagreement estimate while their mean serves as the reward. The test asks whether the spread predicts, on held-out pairs with several annotators, which pairs those annotators split on, and whether the mean keeps the ranking accuracy of a single head on RewardBench.
CONTROLS: A single head trained on all votes with Monte Carlo dropout as its spread, the usual alternative; a model with the same number of heads trained on groups assigned at random, which keeps the architecture and removes the grouping; the correlation of spread with held-out annotator split per pair; ranking accuracy of the mean against the single head; and three seeds.
COST: 360
PREDICTION: The grouped heads should predict held-out splits far better than dropout and than random groups, with the mean within a point of the single head. If random groups predict splits as well, the spread reflects response difficulty and not annotator structure.

TITLE: Bounded per-dimension probabilities combined into one reward
PROPOSAL: We give a reward model four bounded outputs, each the probability that a response meets one dimension of a rubric, correctness, helpfulness, safety and style, and combine them through a learned weighting into the pairwise likelihood used for training on Skywork-Reward-Preference, with the per-dimension outputs anchored by the per-attribute ratings in HelpSteer2. Backbones are Qwen2.5-7B-instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-instruct. We test whether the dimensions explain the final score, whether a fixed threshold on the correctness output separates right from wrong on MATH and a code benchmark, and whether ranking accuracy on RewardBench is kept.
CONTROLS: A single-probability head trained on the same backbone, pairs and anchoring, which isolates the dimensional structure from the rest of the recipe; the four outputs with the anchoring removed, which separates the ratings from the pairs; the correctness output against unit tests and MATH answers at one threshold; ranking accuracy against the single-probability head on RewardBench; and three seeds with bootstrap intervals over prompts.
COST: 520
PREDICTION: The dimensional head should keep ranking accuracy within a point of the single head, and its correctness output at one threshold should separate right from wrong on MATH with high precision. If the unanchored head separates as well, the pairwise data already identify the dimensions.

TITLE: Does an absolute score hold its meaning across prompt difficulty
PROPOSAL: A reward model that outputs the probability that a response is acceptable, trained from Qwen2.5-7B-instruct on UltraFeedback pairs and anchored with a few hundred pointwise ratings, is tested on a set of chat and mathematics prompts stratified by difficulty, where difficulty is the share of policy samples that human raters accept, and the strata are built before any model is run. We tune one acceptance threshold on the whole set and read precision and recall of the accept decision in each difficulty stratum, since a score that encodes only a ranking inside each prompt will accept the best response of a hard prompt that raters reject.
CONTROLS: A scalar Bradley-Terry head with a threshold tuned on the same set; the probabilistic head with its anchoring removed; precision and recall per stratum from the easiest to the hardest prompts, with the hardest stratum holding prompts where raters accept below a tenth of samples; and three seeds with intervals.
COST: 160
PREDICTION: The anchored probabilistic head should keep precision within a few points across strata while the scalar head's precision collapses on hard prompts, where it accepts the least bad response. If the unanchored head holds precision as well, the pairwise loss already encodes absolute quality.

TITLE: A joint likelihood for pairs and ratings against fitting the scale afterwards
PROPOSAL: We model each response's quality as a latent variable on a bounded scale, fit pairwise preferences from Skywork-Reward-Preference through the probability that one latent lies above the other and pointwise ratings from HelpSteer2 through ordinal cut points on the same latent, and train both terms together on Llama-3.1-8B-instruct, so that scarce ratings shape the scale during training instead of after it. The test varies the number of ratings from tens to the full set and asks when joint training pulls ahead of fitting cut points on a frozen pairwise head.
CONTROLS: The two-stage model, a pairwise head followed by cut points fitted on the same ratings, at every rating count; a model trained on ratings alone, which fixes what ratings give without pairs; bin accuracy on held-out ratings and threshold precision on chat, mathematics and code at each count; ranking accuracy on RewardBench, which joint training must keep; and three seeds with bootstrap intervals over rating subsets.
COST: 640
PREDICTION: Joint training should pull ahead at the smallest rating counts and tie the two-stage model once hundreds of ratings are available, with ranking accuracy unchanged. If the two models tie at every count, the scale is identifiable from a frozen head and joint training adds only training time.

TITLE: Stopping Best-of-N early once a calibrated score passes a threshold
PROPOSAL: Candidates are sampled one at a time from a policy on AlpacaEval, MATH and a code benchmark, each is scored by a reward head that outputs a calibrated probability of acceptability, anchored by a few hundred pointwise ratings on Qwen2.5-7B-instruct, and sampling stops as soon as a candidate passes a threshold or when sixteen candidates have been drawn. The question is whether calibration lets the scorer stop early without losing quality, which a ranking-only scorer cannot do because its scores say little about any single candidate on its own.
CONTROLS: The same head without early stopping, picking the best of sixteen, which sets the quality the stopping rule has to keep; a scalar Bradley-Terry head with a threshold on its scores standardised over a held-out pool, the best a ranking-only scorer can do; quality under held-out ratings and unit tests against the average number of candidates drawn; and three seeds.
COST: 440
PREDICTION: Early stopping with the calibrated head should keep the quality of the best of sixteen while drawing under half as many candidates on chat and code, and the standardised scalar head should keep quality only at a far lower stopping rate. If the scalar head stops as early at the same quality, calibration adds little that standardisation does not.
