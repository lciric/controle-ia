TITLE: Per-pixel confidence that tracks accuracy as the query time moves away from the frame
PROPOSAL: We add a confidence output to a causal anytime segmenter that takes the last RGB frame and the events up to the query time, train the confidence with a proper scoring rule on dense labels at sampled offsets, and test whether the reported confidence tracks the true accuracy at every offset in the gap, so that a planner can tell when a prediction is still trustworthy. The confidence head shares the decoder's features. The segmenter uses a frozen image backbone and a recurrent event encoder conditioned on the offset; training runs on driving and drone sequences with labels at sampled offsets.
CONTROLS: Calibration curves are drawn against dense labels at many offsets from a high-rate camera held out from training; the confidence is compared with the softmax maximum of the same segmenter and with a reference curve of accuracy against offset fitted on training sequences; the curves are split by object speed and by pixels that gain or lose an object after the frame; and each run is repeated over seeds.
COST: 240
PREDICTION: Confidence should fall with offset in step with accuracy, fastest on moving objects, and stay above the softmax maximum in calibration at long offsets. If the fitted reference curve matches the learned confidence everywhere, the confidence carries only the offset itself.

TITLE: Continuous-time decoding against binned prediction at offsets never seen in training
PROPOSAL: Training samples offsets from the whole gap, and the decoder receives the query time through a sinusoidal embedding so that one network answers at any instant, not only at a preset list of times. We compare this continuous decoder with a decoder that predicts a stack of maps at twelve evenly spaced offsets and picks the nearest, both on top of the same frozen image backbone and event encoder, trained on driving sequences with dense labels from a high-rate camera. The test asks for maps at offsets drawn between the bin centres and beyond the longest training offset, where the anytime constraint bites.
CONTROLS: Both decoders are scored against dense labels at offsets drawn uniformly inside the gap, including points between bin centres; the binned decoder is also given linear interpolation between its two nearest maps as a stronger reference; a copy of the last frame's map is scored at the same offsets; and accuracy is reported per offset band, repeated over seeds.
COST: 450
PREDICTION: The continuous decoder should keep accuracy flat between bin centres while the binned one dips midway between them, and interpolation should close part of that dip. Failure looks like matching curves at every offset, which means that twelve bins already cover the gap.

TITLE: Simulated event streams with dense labels at every instant as training data for the gap
PROPOSAL: We render driving scenes in a simulator with a physically based event generator, producing RGB frames at a low rate, events in between, and a semantic label at every simulated instant, and we train a causal anytime segmenter on this data with domain randomisation of lighting, contrast thresholds and event noise. The test asks whether a model trained on simulated gaps transfers to real recordings, scored on real dense labels inside the gap from a high-rate camera on the same rig as the event sensor. Fine-tuning on a small real set with labels at frame times only is tried as the practical middle path.
CONTROLS: The simulated model is compared with a model trained on real sequences carrying dense in-gap labels, which fixes what real supervision gives; with a model trained on real sequences labelled at frame times only; and with the simulated model after short real fine-tuning; all are scored on the same real held-out gaps at matched offsets, and the runs are repeated over seeds.
COST: 520
PREDICTION: The simulated model should land between the frame-time model and the dense real model at long offsets, and fine-tuning should close most of the remaining distance. If the simulated model trails the frame-time model everywhere, the event generator's noise does not resemble the sensor's.

TITLE: Does the event stream add more than motion extrapolated from the last frame
PROPOSAL: We train a causal anytime segmenter with the last RGB frame and the events up to the query time, and we set it against a model that receives the last frame plus the events of only a short slice right after it, from which a velocity field is estimated and then extrapolated to the query time. The question is whether the events arriving late in the gap carry information that extrapolation cannot supply, which is the case for objects that turn, stop or enter after the frame. Both models share the backbone, the decoder and the training recipe on driving sequences with dense labels from a high-rate camera.
CONTROLS: The extrapolation model with an optical-flow field estimated on the last two frames in place of the event slice; the full model with its late events replaced by a constant-velocity continuation of the early slice; accuracy on pixels where an object changes course or appears after the frame against pixels that keep moving as before; and the same runs repeated over seeds.
COST: 180
PREDICTION: The full model should lead the extrapolation models on course-changing and appearing objects and tie with them on steady motion. If the two curves stay together everywhere, the late events are not being read and the method is extrapolation in disguise.

TITLE: Distilling a non-causal teacher into a causal anytime student
PROPOSAL: A teacher that sees both frames around the gap and the events, and so can interpolate, is trained first; a causal student that sees only the first frame and the events up to the query time is then trained to match the teacher's soft maps at sampled offsets alongside the labels. The claim under test is that the teacher's view of the future improves the student at long offsets, where labels are scarce, without any leak of future information into the student at run time. Backbones are shared, and the data are driving and quadruped sequences with dense labels.
CONTROLS: The student trained on labels alone; the teacher itself, scored at the same offsets as a non-causal reference; a leak check in which the student is run with the events after the query time deleted and with them scrambled, which must leave its output unchanged; distillation from a teacher that is itself causal, to separate soft targets from the future view; and seeds repeated.
COST: 400
PREDICTION: The distilled student should gain most at the longest offsets and on objects that appear after the frame, while the causal-teacher variant gains less. If the leak check changes the output, the student is reading the future and the claim is void.

TITLE: Distilling a non-causal teacher into a causal anytime student across teacher widths
PROPOSAL: Backbones are shared, and the data are driving and quadruped sequences with dense labels. A teacher that sees both frames around the gap and the events, and so can interpolate, is trained first at several widths; a causal student that sees only the first frame and the events up to the query time is then trained to match each teacher's soft maps at sampled offsets alongside the labels. The claim under test is that a wider teacher yields a better student at the offsets where the teacher's maps are sharpest, and that the softmax temperature of the soft targets sets how much of that gain survives.
CONTROLS: The student distilled from the narrowest teacher alone; each teacher itself, scored at the same offsets as a reference for its width; a temperature check in which the soft targets are produced at a low and at a high temperature, which must change the student's sharpness; distillation with the loss weight on the soft maps halved, to separate target sharpness from weighting; and seeds repeated.
COST: 400
PREDICTION: The widest teacher should give the student the largest gain at short offsets and on objects visible in the frame, while the low-temperature variant gains less. If the temperature check changes little, the student is reading the labels and the claim is void.
