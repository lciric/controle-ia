=== D 1
ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Label correction as a sequential decision problem solved by reinforcement learning
PROPOSAL: We cast label correction as a Markov decision process in which a correction agent observes a training sample's features, current label and the classifier's predictive distribution, and chooses to keep the label or replace it with another class. The reward is the change in the classifier's accuracy on a small trusted validation split after a training interval, so the agent learns from delayed consequences. We train the agent with an actor-critic method on image benchmarks with synthetic and real noise.
CONTROLS: Training on noisy labels, small-loss sample selection, label correction by the model's own predictions, and a semi-supervised method that treats suspected noisy samples as unlabelled, at noise rates from mild to severe.
COST: 380
PREDICTION: The agent should outperform greedy self-correction at high noise rates, where early mistakes compound. The trusted split may be too small to give a stable reward signal at the largest class counts.
=== D 2
ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Exploration in label correction through stochastic relabelling policies
PROPOSAL: We compare deterministic relabelling, which always picks the most probable class, with stochastic relabelling that samples a class from a learned policy and is rewarded by downstream accuracy over many epochs. The point is to test whether exploring alternative corrections avoids locking in early errors under instance-dependent noise. Experiments use image classification benchmarks with feature-dependent synthetic noise and two datasets with real annotation noise.
CONTROLS: Deterministic relabelling with the same features, stochastic relabelling with a fixed temperature but no learning, and no correction, each with five seeds.
COST: 260
PREDICTION: Learned stochastic relabelling should yield higher clean test accuracy than deterministic relabelling at severe noise. Its variance across seeds may be larger, which we will report alongside the mean.

TITLE: Benchmarking label correction under instance-dependent noise of increasing severity
PROPOSAL: We generate instance-dependent noise whose flip probability grows with similarity to other classes in a pretrained feature space, at rates from ten to fifty percent, and evaluate existing correction, selection, reweighting and robust-loss methods alongside a learned correction policy. Beyond final accuracy, we measure the precision and recall of the corrected labels against the true ones over training.
CONTROLS: Class-conditional noise at the same rates for comparison, clean-label training as the ceiling, and training on noisy labels as the floor.
COST: 300
PREDICTION: Methods relying on small-loss selection should degrade sharply under instance-dependent noise, while correction methods that use features should degrade more gently. Learned policies may need more validation data than the others.

PROPOSAL: We let a learned correction policy decide, for each suspected noisy sample, among keeping the label, relabelling it, or discarding the label so that the sample joins an unlabelled pool used with consistency regularisation. TITLE: Letting a correction policy revise its own earlier relabelling decisions
PROPOSAL: We train a label-correction agent that visits every training sample once per epoch and may keep, change or restore its label, so a correction made early can be undone once the classifier has learned more, in contrast with policies that commit once. The agent sees the sample's features, its current and original labels and the classifier's predictive distribution, and receives as reward the change in accuracy on a small trusted split over the following epoch. We run it on image benchmarks with instance-dependent noise at severe rates and report clean test accuracy and the precision and recall of the final labels against the true ones, together with the number of reversals made in each epoch.
CONTROLS: A comparison with the same agent allowed a single decision per sample at matched decisions per epoch and with greedy correction by the classifier's own predictions, a reference run with clean labels, the same setting repeated with the trusted split varied in size, and final label precision measured against the true labels at every epoch, over five seeds.
COST: 300
PREDICTION: The revising agent should end with higher clean accuracy and higher label precision than the commit-once agent, with most reversals happening in the middle of training. A null result is a precision curve that the commit-once agent matches at every epoch, or reversals that are as often wrong as right.

TITLE: Exploration guided by feature neighbours for instance-dependent label noise
PROPOSAL: Because instance-dependent noise flips labels toward classes that look alike in feature space, we give a stochastic correction policy an exploration bonus for trying, on a suspected sample, the labels held by its nearest neighbours in a pretrained feature space, and we decay the bonus as those labels are tried. The policy is rewarded by the accuracy change on a trusted split over the next epoch. We test on image benchmarks with instance-dependent noise built from feature similarity at rates up to half the labels, where the confusable classes are exactly the neighbours, and report clean accuracy and correction precision.
CONTROLS: A comparison with a uniform exploration bonus over all classes at matched exploration rate and with the policy without any bonus, a reference run with greedy correction by the classifier's predictions, the same setting repeated with class-conditional noise at the same rates for contrast, and correction precision measured against the true labels in the confusable class pairs, over five seeds.
COST: 290
PREDICTION: The neighbour bonus should lift precision in confusable pairs beyond the uniform bonus at severe instance-dependent rates, and the two bonuses should tie under class-conditional noise. The idea is refuted if the uniform bonus matches it under instance-dependent noise, or if precision in confusable pairs does not move.

ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Does a correction policy need the training history of each sample
PROPOSAL: We compare a correction policy that reads, for each sample, the sequence of its losses, predicted classes and confidences over all past epochs through a small recurrent network, with a policy that reads only the current epoch's values, both trained with the same delayed reward from a trusted split. The hypothesis is that long-term decisions need the trajectory, since samples that were learned late and forgotten often carry wrong labels. Both policies run on image benchmarks with instance-dependent noise at high rates, and we record clean test accuracy, correction precision and the epochs at which corrections occur, splitting every metric between samples learned early and samples learned late.
CONTROLS: A comparison with the history-free policy at matched parameter count and with a policy given the history truncated to the last few epochs, a reference run with greedy correction from the current prediction, the same setting repeated with the history length varied from one epoch to all of them, and correction precision measured against the true labels for samples learned late, over five seeds.
COST: 170
PREDICTION: The full-history policy should gain most on samples learned late and lose little on samples learned early, with precision rising with history length up to the full trajectory. The hypothesis is contradicted if the truncated history matches the full one, or if precision on late-learned samples stays flat across lengths.
