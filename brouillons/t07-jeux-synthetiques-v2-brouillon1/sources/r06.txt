TITLE: Position-dependent drafting without training, a horizontal cascade of self-draft and lookup
PROPOSAL: We test the horizontal idea that the first positions of a draft block deserve the more accurate drafter. Within each speculation round, the opening draft tokens come from an early-exit self-draft that runs the target model up to an intermediate layer and reuses the final normalisation and unembedding, and the remaining tokens of the block come from n-gram lookup in the prompt and generated prefix. We move the split point from the start to the end of the block on Vicuna-7B and Llama-3-8B-instruct over the six Spec-Bench categories and we measure speedup over autoregressive decoding together with the mean number of accepted tokens per round.
CONTROLS: The comparison includes early-exit drafting alone and lookup alone at the same total block length, the reversed arrangement where lookup drafts the opening positions, a cascade whose drafter is chosen at random per position, and a sweep of the block length so that the best fixed schedule for each single drafter is known before the mixed schedule is judged, with outputs checked against greedy decoding.
COST: 56
PREDICTION: We expect the mixed schedule to beat both single drafters on chat and reasoning, with the best split point around a third of the block, and to add nothing on retrieval-augmented generation where lookup accepts long runs anyway. The reversed arrangement should be worse than either single drafter, which would confirm that early positions matter most.

PROPOSAL: We profile prompt lookup, layer skipping and early exit on Llama-2-7B-chat and Llama-3-8B-instruct on a data-centre card, an older data-centre card and a consumer card, recording acceptance and latency relative to a target forward pass, then select for each card the cascade arrangement and draft lengths that the expected-walltime model ranks first, and measure the speedup of each selected schedule on its own card and on the other cards over Spec-Bench.
CONTROLS: Lookup alone and autoregressive decoding on every card, a brute-force grid per card to check the model's choice, fixed kernels and precision across cards, repeated timings with warm caches, and identity of outputs with greedy decoding.
COST: 72
PREDICTION: We expect the self-draft level to pay on the consumer card, where memory bandwidth dominates and skipping layers saves proportionally more, and not on the fastest card. A schedule transferred from another card should lose a modest share of the speedup, most when moving to the consumer card.

TITLE: Fused verification kernels to shorten the target's forward pass in cascades
PROPOSAL: We profile the verification pass of Llama-2-7B-chat and Llama-3-8B-instruct inside a cascade of layer skipping above prompt lookup on a data-centre card, an older data-centre card and a consumer card, attribute its latency to attention, feed-forward and sampling kernels, and then implement a fused attention-and-sampling kernel for the tree-shaped verification of draft blocks together with a cache layout that avoids re-copying rejected positions. We measure the latency of the verification pass per draft length and the resulting end-to-end speedup over autoregressive decoding across Spec-Bench.
CONTROLS: The unfused verification pass on every card, the cascade with the original cache layout, repeated timings with warm caches, fixed precision across cards, and identity of outputs with greedy decoding.
COST: 72
PREDICTION: We expect the fused kernel to shorten verification by a noticeable fraction on the consumer card and less on the data-centre cards, and the end-to-end speedup to follow. The gain should be the same whatever the drafter above lookup.

TITLE: Rounds and draft lengths of a three-level cascade, model-guided search then online refinement
PROPOSAL: We search the schedule of a three-level cascade, early exit above layer skipping above prompt lookup, on Llama-3-8B-instruct and Qwen2-7B-instruct: the expected-walltime model proposes draft lengths and the number of lower-level rounds per upper round from measured per-level acceptance and latency, and a per-request tracker then nudges the lengths from observed acceptance during generation. We measure walltime speedup over autoregressive decoding across Spec-Bench and report the gap between model-chosen, refined and brute-force schedules.
CONTROLS: Lookup alone and autoregressive decoding, the model-chosen schedule without refinement, a brute-force grid as the ceiling, a tracker fed random acceptance as placebo, repeated timings, and identity of outputs with greedy decoding.
COST: 24
PREDICTION: We expect the model-chosen schedule to land within a tenth of the grid optimum and the tracker to close half of the remaining gap on categories where acceptance drifts within responses. If refinement does nothing, fixed schedules suffice for the second question.

TITLE: A theoretical bound on retrieval-drafter acceptance from context repetition statistics
PROPOSAL: We derive an upper bound on the expected acceptance rate of prompt lookup drafting as a function of the conditional entropy of the next token given a matched n-gram, estimated from the empirical n-gram statistics of a corpus, and a matching lower bound for sources with bounded repetition. We then estimate the bound's inputs from the prompts and reference outputs of each Spec-Bench category, without running any model, and from two general corpora, and we tabulate the predicted acceptance per category and n-gram order for the Llama-3-8B-instruct and Qwen2-7B-instruct tokenisations.
CONTROLS: Bounds computed under two estimators of conditional entropy, with and without smoothing, a check that the bound is tight on a synthetic source with known repetition, and the same quantities computed on shuffled corpora as a floor.
COST: 24
PREDICTION: We expect the predicted acceptance to be highest on summarisation and retrieval-augmented generation and lowest on chat and reasoning. The bound should be close to tight on the synthetic source and loose on natural text.
