TITLE: Per-instance view information gain estimated with a conditional entropy bound
PROPOSAL: We quantify, for each training image, how much a given augmented view adds beyond what the model already encodes, using a conditional entropy estimate computed from the embedding of the anchor and of the view under a small variational decoder trained alongside a SimCLR encoder. We train a ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 with the standard augmentation set, log the per-instance gain for each augmentation family across training, and relate it to the final linear probe accuracy of the classes those instances belong to. The output is a map from augmentation family and training stage to estimated information contributed, which we then use to reweight augmentation sampling in a second run.
CONTROLS: The same encoder with uniform augmentation sampling, reweighting by a random per-instance score with the same distribution, reweighting by the InfoNCE loss of the pair instead of the entropy estimate, and a frozen-encoder variant of the estimator to check that the gain signal is not an artefact of co-training, three seeds each.
COST: 260
PREDICTION: Gains should be largest early in training and for colour-based augmentations, and reweighting should improve linear probe accuracy on CIFAR-100 by around one point at batch 256. If reweighting by a random score matches it, the estimator carries no instance-specific signal.

TITLE: Comparing mutual information estimators as predictors of what each view contributes
PROPOSAL: We ask whether the mutual information (MI) between two views of the same instance, estimated per instance, predicts how useful that instance's augmentations are for representation quality. We train a residual network (ResNet) with 18 layers using the simple framework for contrastive learning of visual representations (SimCLR) on the CIFAR-100 dataset from the Canadian Institute for Advanced Research and on a 100-class subset of ImageNet, and we obtain three per-instance MI estimates during training: the information noise-contrastive estimation (InfoNCE) bound, a nearest-neighbour entropy estimate in embedding space, and a variational bound with a learned critic. We then remove augmentations for the instances in the top and bottom deciles of each estimate and measure the change in linear probe and k-nearest-neighbour (kNN) accuracy.
CONTROLS: Removal of augmentations for random deciles of the same size, removal guided by loss value rather than MI, and the three estimators evaluated on a frozen encoder at the end of training, with three seeds per setting.
COST: 300
PREDICTION: Instances in the bottom decile of estimated MI should be the ones whose augmentation removal hurts least, with the variational bound ranking instances most consistently across seeds. If random deciles produce the same change, per-instance MI carries no actionable information.

TITLE: Small-batch contrastive learning with per-instance adaptive temperature driven by view agreement
PROPOSAL: We test whether the trade-off between invariance and expressivity can be managed per instance by setting the InfoNCE temperature of each anchor from the agreement between its views: anchors whose views already agree closely receive a lower temperature that sharpens separation from neighbours, while anchors whose views disagree receive a higher temperature that tolerates the spread. Agreement is the cosine similarity between view embeddings under a momentum encoder. We train ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 encoders on CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet-100 at batch sizes 32, 128 and 512 and measure linear probe accuracy, kNN accuracy, alignment and uniformity.
CONTROLS: A fixed temperature tuned per batch size, a global temperature schedule that decays over training, a temperature assigned from a shuffled agreement vector, and the same adaptive rule applied to a MoCo variant with a queue so that the effect is not specific to in-batch negatives, each over three seeds.
COST: 480
PREDICTION: Adaptive temperature should help most at batch 32, narrowing the gap to batch 512 by roughly a third on ImageNet-100, and should give nothing at batch 512. If the shuffled assignment performs the same, the gain comes from the temperature distribution rather than from instance-level matching.

TITLE: Does the augmentation graph spectrum tell us when more views stop helping
PROPOSAL: We examine the claim that contrastive learning performs spectral clustering on an augmentation graph by measuring, on real datasets, how the spectrum of the empirical augmentation kernel changes as augmentation strength increases, and whether the eigenvalue decay predicts the information that further views add. We estimate the augmentation graph on CIFAR-10 and STL-10 by sampling 64 views per image for a subset of 5000 images, embed them with a trained SimCLR ResNet-18, extract the top eigenvalues of the induced similarity matrix at four augmentation strengths, and correlate the eigengap with the marginal gain in linear probe accuracy obtained when the number of views per instance per epoch is doubled.
CONTROLS: Embeddings from a randomly initialised encoder and from a supervised encoder, a kernel built from pixel-space distances instead of embeddings, and shuffled pairings between eigengap and accuracy gain to calibrate the correlation.
COST: 90
PREDICTION: A larger eigengap should coincide with a smaller marginal gain from extra views, giving a usable criterion for when more views stop helping. The correlation may vanish if the subset is too small to estimate the spectrum reliably.

PROPOSAL: We allocate the number of augmented views drawn per instance in each epoch according to a running estimate of how much its recent views disagreed in embedding space, giving more views to instances whose views still carry fresh information and fewer to instances whose views have converged.
