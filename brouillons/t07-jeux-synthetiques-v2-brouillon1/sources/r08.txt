TITLE: Likelihood-shift scores for context use and memory use in retrieval-augmented answers
PROPOSAL: We define two scores that need no choice of attention heads or layers. The context score is the gain in log-likelihood of the generated answer when the retrieved documents are present compared with when they are replaced by unrelated documents of matched length. The memory score is the log-likelihood of the same answer under a closed-book prompt relative to the likelihood of a model-free reference answer. Both are read from the output distribution of any white-box model, so they can be computed by a scorer that is not the generator. We evaluate the difference between the memory score and the context score as a hallucination detector on RAGTruth across its question answering, summarization and data-to-text splits, on a post-cutoff news question set we assemble, and with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as generators and scorers in every combination.
CONTROLS: ReDeEP with its published head and layer selection, SelfCheckGPT, AlignScore, a prompted judge, and raw answer perplexity, all reported with AUROC and precision-recall area; a placebo in which the context score is computed against shuffled documents; detectors fitted on one dataset and tested on the others; three seeds for every sampled quantity.
COST: 180
PREDICTION: The difference score should match ReDeEP on the dataset it was tuned on and beat it off-distribution, with AUROC above the prompted judge on question answering. It could fail where summaries paraphrase heavily, since likelihood shifts then track wording rather than grounding.
=== D 2
ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Behavioral reliance scores from counterfactual document edits
PROPOSAL: We measure context use behaviorally rather than through internal activations. For each query we create edited copies of the retrieved documents in which one fact that the answer depends on is replaced by a plausible alternative, using a span-replacement model. The context score of an answer is the probability, estimated under the generator with sampling, that the answer follows the edited fact when the edit is present. The memory score is the probability that the generator reproduces its original claim under a closed-book prompt. An answer whose memory score exceeds its context score is flagged. The procedure needs only sampling access and runs on API models too. We test on RAGTruth and on a fresh news question set with answers dated after the training cutoff of Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
CONTROLS: ReDeEP and the semantic entropy probe as white-box references on the open models, SelfCheckGPT and a prompted judge as black-box references, a placebo edit that changes a fact the answer does not depend on, and an ablation that scores with a scorer model different from the generator, with bootstrap confidence intervals over queries.
COST: 240
PREDICTION: Behavioral scores should reach AUROC within a few points of ReDeEP on RAGTruth and transfer to the news set without any refitting, where ReDeEP degrades. If edits are too easy to detect as out of place, the generator may ignore them regardless of grounding, which would flatten the context score and break the detector.

TITLE: Layer-agnostic utilisation scores pooled across the whole residual stream
PROPOSAL: Rather than selecting layers, we compute at every layer the cosine similarity between the representation of each generated token and the attention-weighted summary of the context tokens, and the divergence between the representation entering and leaving the feed-forward block, then pool both quantities over layers with a fixed, uniform weighting and over the answer with a maximum. The resulting context score and memory score are therefore defined for any decoder-only model without tuning, and we characterise how much the uniform pooling loses against the tuned selection. We evaluate hallucination detection on RAGTruth and on a long-form summarisation set built from recent news, using Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B-it, and we optimise nothing on the test datasets.
CONTROLS: ReDeEP with its tuned heads and layers as the reference, the same pooled scores with a random subset of layers, each score alone, raw perplexity as the floor, a prompted judge, and transfer of a threshold fitted on one model to the other two; three seeds on sampled generations and bootstrap intervals on AUROC.
COST: 150
PREDICTION: Uniform pooling should lose at most a few points of AUROC against the tuned selection on the tuning dataset and gain on the other datasets and models. The approach could fail if the memory signal is concentrated in a narrow band of layers, in which case uniform pooling would dilute it below the detection threshold.

TITLE: Validating utilization scores on documents with planted facts
PROPOSAL: Existing scores are checked only by correlation with hallucination labels, never by whether they track use of the documents. We build a validation suite in which reliance is known by construction. They may fail on the contradiction part if the models refuse or hedge, producing answers that follow neither source.
=== D 6
ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Claim-level grounding and recall scores as a black-box utilization detector
PROPOSAL: We decompose each generated answer into atomic claims with a claim splitter, then score every claim twice. The context score of a claim is the entailment probability given the retrieved documents from an off-the-shelf verifier such as MiniCheck. The memory score of a claim is the probability that the generator asserts the same claim when asked the question without documents, estimated from ten closed-book samples with a claim matcher. An answer is scored by the largest gap between memory score and context score over its claims, so no attention head, layer or model internals enter the computation, and the generator may be any model with sampling access. We run the detector on RAGTruth with Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and on a data-to-text split where tables are the documents.
CONTROLS: ReDeEP and the semantic entropy probe on the open models, the verifier alone without the memory score, the memory score alone, SelfCheckGPT, a prompted judge, a placebo that matches closed-book samples to claims from a different answer, and three seeds for the sampled closed-book answers.
COST: 160
PREDICTION: The gap score should beat the verifier alone by several AUROC points on question answering, because unsupported claims that the model also states closed-book are the typical hallucination. The detector could fail on data-to-text, where closed-book sampling yields nothing comparable and the memory score collapses to zero for every claim.

TITLE: Next-token distribution shifts as head-free utilization scores
PROPOSAL: We compare, at every generated position, the next-token distribution of the model given the query and documents with the distribution given the query alone, and with the distribution given the documents alone. The context score of the answer is the sum over positions of the divergence between the full prompt and the query-only prompt, which measures how much the documents move the prediction. The memory score is the sum over positions of the log-probability the query-only prompt assigns to the chosen token, which measures how much the answer was already available without documents. Both quantities are read from the output layer, so no attention head or intermediate layer is selected, and any model that exposes logits can be scored. We evaluate on RAGTruth and on a news summarization set collected after the cutoff of Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct.
CONTROLS: ReDeEP with its published settings, a semantic entropy probe, AlignScore, a prompted judge, each score alone, a placebo computing the divergence against a prompt with shuffled documents, cross-dataset thresholds, and bootstrap intervals over three seeds of sampled answers.
COST: 90
PREDICTION: The combined score should match ReDeEP on RAGTruth question answering and transfer better to summarization, where head selection was tuned on a different distribution. We expect failure when answers are long and formulaic, because divergence accumulates on function tokens and swamps the few grounded content tokens.

TITLE: Semantic entropy under open-book and closed-book sampling as utilization signals
PROPOSAL: We sample ten answers per query under the full prompt and ten under a closed-book prompt, cluster each set by bidirectional entailment, and define the memory score as the probability mass of the closed-book cluster that contains the original answer and the context score as the drop in semantic entropy from the closed-book set to the open-book set. An answer whose memory score is high while the context score is low is flagged as relying on memory. The procedure needs only sampling, so it scores closed models as well as Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and we compare generator-side sampling with sampling from a separate open model acting as a surrogate. Datasets are RAGTruth and a summarization set of recent news articles paired with human labels we collect.
CONTROLS: ReDeEP and the semantic entropy probe on the open models, plain semantic entropy of the open-book set without the memory score, SelfCheckGPT, a prompted judge, a placebo with documents drawn from another query, three seeds for the sampling, and bootstrap intervals.
COST: 300
PREDICTION: The two-score detector should beat plain semantic entropy on RAGTruth and keep most of its AUROC on closed generators through the surrogate. It may fail on summarization, where clustering by entailment fragments long outputs into many singleton clusters and the entropy estimate becomes noise.

ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Sentence-dropout regression for a head-free context utilization score
PROPOSAL: We estimate how much each retrieved sentence contributes to an answer by randomly dropping sentences from the documents and regressing the answer log-likelihood on the presence indicators, which yields additive contributions without any choice of attention heads or layers. The context score is the sum of positive contributions, and the memory score is the intercept of the regression, which is the likelihood the model assigns to the answer when nothing from the documents is present. The ratio of memory score to context score is the detector. Scoring runs with the generator or with a different open model, and we test both on RAGTruth and on a question answering set about events after the cutoff of Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct.
CONTROLS: ReDeEP and the semantic entropy probe, AlignScore, a prompted judge, a leave-one-sentence-out variant with no regression, each score alone, a placebo where the dropped sentences are replaced by sentences from another document, three seeds for the dropout masks, and bootstrap intervals over queries.
COST: 220
PREDICTION: The regression score should beat the leave-one-out variant and match ReDeEP in-distribution while transferring to the post-cutoff set without refitting. It could fail when the answer is grounded in a single sentence, because dropout then produces nearly binary likelihoods and the regression becomes ill-conditioned.

TITLE: Grounding versus recall scores for detecting hallucinated answers
PROPOSAL: We measure utilization at the level of content units. Named entities, numbers and dates in the answer are extracted and each is labelled as grounded when it appears, after normalization, in the retrieved documents, and as recalled when the generator produces it in closed-book answers to the same query. Because the features are extracted from text alone, the detector applies to any generator, including closed models, and never touches attention heads or layers. COST: 60
PREDICTION: The combined detector should beat each fraction alone and approach ReDeEP on question answering while staying usable on the closed model. It should fail on abstractive summaries with few extractable units, where both fractions are undefined for many answers.
