TITLE: Personalising a multi-view diffusion model on the input before lifting to 3D
PROPOSAL: We fine-tune a pretrained multi-view diffusion model on augmented crops and rotations of the single input image of the subject, using low-rank adapters, and add a few synthetic views produced by a personalised 2D model of the same subject. The adapted model then generates the full set of views that a native 3D reconstructor turns into an asset. We evaluate identity preservation on side and back views with an identity embedding model and human preference, on a benchmark of pets, toys and figurines with ground-truth multi-view captures.
CONTROLS: The unadapted multi-view model, score distillation with a personalised 2D prior, and a native 3D generator conditioned on the same image.
COST: 480
PREDICTION: Personalised multi-view generation should raise identity similarity on unseen views without hurting view consistency. Very thin structures may still be reconstructed poorly by the 3D stage.

TITLE: Measuring identity preservation across viewpoints for generated 3D assets
PROPOSAL: We build an evaluation protocol for subject-driven 3D generation from captures of real subjects photographed from all sides. The generator receives one front view, and its renders at the held-out angles are compared with the real photographs using identity embeddings from several feature extractors, colour histogram distances and pairwise human judgements. We report scores by angle to show where identity is lost.
CONTROLS: Agreement between the automatic scores and the human judgements, scores of the real photographs against each other as an upper bound, and scores of a different subject of the same category as a lower bound.
COST: 90
PREDICTION: Existing generators should lose identity mostly at back views, with colour shifts as a common failure. Feature extractors trained on faces may misjudge animal subjects.
=== D 3
ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Correcting colour bias of multi-view diffusion with a subject-specific palette prior
PROPOSAL: We estimate a colour palette and texture statistics from the input image of the subject and add a guidance term during multi-view sampling that penalises deviations from these statistics in the generated views. This targets the systematic colour errors observed on unseen sides. We test on pets and objects whose back views share the front palette and on subjects whose back differs, to check that the prior does not erase real differences.
CONTROLS: Sampling without guidance, guidance with a palette from a different subject, and human ratings of colour faithfulness on held-out real views.
COST: 210
PREDICTION: Palette guidance should cut colour errors for uniform subjects. Subjects with distinct back patterns may be wrongly homogenised.

PROPOSAL: We optimise a single text-token embedding in the conditioning space of a native image-to-3D generator so that renders of the generated asset, taken at the input viewpoint, match the input photograph in a perceptual and identity-embedding loss, with the generator weights frozen and gradients flowing through its differentiable renderer. PREDICTION: The learned token should cut identity distance on back views and win the paired preference, while the other-subject token should sit near the caption run. If the other-subject token gains as much, the token carries category appearance and not the subject.

PROPOSAL: We predict dense part correspondences on the generated mesh, between the visible surface and its symmetric or repeated counterparts, and transfer observed texture along them before an inpainting network fills the regions left without a counterpart, so that the back of a subject inherits its real colours wherever geometry permits.
