TITLE: Iterated rejection sampling as KL-regularised reward fine-tuning for diffusion models
PROPOSAL: We show that repeatedly fine-tuning a diffusion model on its own reward-filtered samples, where each round accepts a sample with probability proportional to the exponentiated reward divided by a running bound, converges to the reference distribution tilted by the reward, so the marginal KL term never has to be evaluated. We test this on a latent text-to-image model with an aesthetic reward and on a molecule generator with a docking proxy, tracking reward, sample diversity and a held-out estimate of KL from importance weights.
CONTROLS: Proximal policy optimisation without KL, trajectory-KL fine-tuning, best-of-N at a matched number of samples, and an ablation of the acceptance bound.
COST: 640
PREDICTION: Reward should rise steadily while diversity stays close to the reference model, unlike the unregularised baseline. The guarantee may break when the acceptance rate falls too low for steep rewards.

TITLE: Splitting the denoising chain into stages for reward fine-tuning
PROPOSAL: We fine-tune a pretrained diffusion model one segment of the sampling trajectory at a time, starting from the final denoising steps and moving backwards, so that each stage solves a short-horizon KL-regularised problem whose solution is the previous stage tilted by an intermediate reward estimate. The intermediate rewards come from a learned value model on noisy states. We evaluate on text-to-image alignment with a preference reward and on layout generation with a constraint-satisfaction reward.
CONTROLS: We compare against fine-tuning all steps jointly with the same reward, against a version without the value model that propagates the final reward directly, and against the frozen reference model, reporting reward, Fréchet distance and an importance-weighted KL estimate for each.
COST: 900
PREDICTION: Staged fine-tuning should reach the joint method's reward with fewer reward queries and less drift from the reference. If the value model on noisy states is poor, early stages will tilt toward the wrong regions and the advantage will vanish.

ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Best-of-many distillation as a tractable route to regularised reward tuning
PROPOSAL: We draw many samples per prompt from a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model, keep the top-scoring one under a preference reward, and fine-tune on the kept samples. We prove that the distribution of the selected sample has a closed-form Kullback-Leibler divergence to the reference model that depends only on the number of candidates, so the regularisation strength becomes a single integer that the practitioner sets. We then compare the trade-off between reward and divergence reached by this procedure with policy-gradient methods across three prompt sets.
CONTROLS: Policy-gradient fine-tuning without regularisation, policy-gradient fine-tuning with a trajectory-level divergence penalty, and the untouched reference model, all evaluated with Fréchet Inception Distance and human preference win rates.
COST: 520
PREDICTION: The selection-based model should match policy-gradient reward at a known divergence, with more stable training. The bound may be loose when many candidates have nearly equal reward.

TITLE: Reward tilting through noise-level importance resampling
PROPOSAL: We treat each intermediate noisy state of a diffusion sampler as a particle and resample particles at several noise levels according to a look-ahead reward estimate, then fine-tune the model on the resulting trajectories. Because the resampling weights telescope, the fine-tuned model targets the reward-tilted reference distribution without needing marginal likelihoods. We apply it to an open latent diffusion model trained on public image-text pairs and to a graph diffusion model for small molecules scored by a drug-likeness reward.
CONTROLS: Fine-tuning on final-step resampling only, sequential Monte Carlo at inference with no fine-tuning, and trajectory-penalised policy gradient, compared on reward, diversity and estimated divergence.
COST: 760
PREDICTION: Intermediate resampling should reach a given reward with less divergence than final-step resampling. Weight degeneracy at high noise levels could erase the benefit for sharp rewards.

TITLE: Reward fine-tuning of flow models through straight-path surrogates
PROPOSAL: We exploit the deterministic sampling path of flow models by fine-tuning on reward-weighted pairs of noise and endpoint samples, regressing the velocity field towards the straight line between them. Since the pairs are weighted by the exponentiated reward, we show that the result is the reference flow tilted by the reward up to a coupling error that we bound. We run this on a class-conditional image flow and on a molecular conformer flow, with rewards for aesthetic score and a binding affinity proxy.

COST: 380
PREDICTION: We expect stable reward gains with less diversity loss than reward backpropagation. If the coupling error grows with temperature, high-reward settings will drift away from the tilted target.

TITLE: Value-guided multi-step fine-tuning for diffusion rewards
PROPOSAL: We train a value network on noisy intermediate states to predict the expected final reward, then fine-tune the denoiser step by step so that each transition is the reference transition reweighted by the exponentiated change in value.
