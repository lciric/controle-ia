TITLE: Measuring routing consistency of image latent patches in DiT-MoE
PROPOSAL: We train DiT-B/2 with token-choice MoE layers of eight experts on ImageNet at 256 resolution and record, for every latent patch, the expert chosen at each layer across denoising timesteps, across neighbouring patches, and across samples of the same class. We compute routing entropy, the mutual information between expert choice and timestep, spatial position and class, and the overlap of expert token sets, and we repeat the same analysis on a language MoE of similar size trained on text to compare how concentrated the routing is.
CONTROLS: A dense DiT-B/2 with the same activated parameters, the same MoE with a fixed random router, DiffMoE-style batch-level routing, and expert-choice routing, all trained for the same number of samples and scored by FID and sFID with the same sampler.
COST: 1200
PREDICTION: Routing of image patches should be far less consistent than for text tokens, with most mutual information on timestep and position and little on content. If the random router matches the learned one in FID, the learned router carries no specialisation at all.

TITLE: Timestep and frequency conditioned routing for diffusion transformers
PROPOSAL: We design a router that receives, besides the token, an embedding of the denoising timestep and the local spatial-frequency energy of the patch, and that assigns tokens to experts through a two-level choice: first a group by noise level, then an expert within the group by content. We train DiT-L/2 with eight experts on ImageNet at 256 resolution, matching the activated parameters of the dense model, and we measure expert specialisation as the consistency of expert assignment for the same patch across adjacent timesteps and as the divergence between expert input distributions.
CONTROLS: Dense DiT-L/2 with matched activated parameters and with matched total parameters, token-choice DiT-MoE, expert-choice EC-DiT and DiffMoE, all trained under the same schedule and sampler, plus the two-level router with the content level replaced by a random choice.
COST: 2400
PREDICTION: The conditioned router should beat token-choice routing in FID and show far higher assignment consistency. If the random content level does as well, specialisation comes only from the noise-level grouping.

TITLE: Scaling experts and model size under a specialisation-promoting router
PROPOSAL: We test whether a router trained with an auxiliary loss that rewards non-overlapping expert inputs keeps improving over dense DiTs as the model and the number of experts grow. The loss pushes apart the mean input features of different experts and penalises tokens whose top two expert scores are close. We train DiT-S/2, DiT-B/2 and DiT-L/2 with four, eight and sixteen experts on ImageNet at 256 resolution, with activated parameters held equal to each dense model, and report FID, sFID and precision and recall at each point of the grid.
CONTROLS: Dense models at each size, token-choice routing with the standard load-balancing loss only, expert-choice routing, a random fixed router, and all variants trained for equal numbers of samples with the same sampler and guidance scale.
COST: 3600
PREDICTION: The gap over dense should widen with model size and expert count for the specialisation loss, while token-choice routing should stay flat or shrink. If the random router scales equally well, capacity rather than routing explains the gains.

TITLE: Mixture-of-experts layers in the latent autoencoder of a diffusion pipeline
PROPOSAL: We place token-choice MoE layers in the decoder of the latent autoencoder that a DiT-XL/2 samples into, with eight experts on the decoder's transformer blocks, and train the autoencoder on ImageNet at 256 resolution with the diffusion model kept fixed. Since the decoder sees nearly clean latents, we expect its tokens to be more separable than noisy diffusion tokens, and we measure whether reconstruction and the final sampled image quality improve at equal activated parameters.
CONTROLS: The dense decoder at equal activated parameters and at equal total parameters, a decoder with experts assigned by random fixed routing, and the original public autoencoder, scored by reconstruction PSNR and a perceptual metric, by FID of samples decoded through each decoder, and by expert input-set overlap, with three seeds.
COST: 2600
PREDICTION: The sparse decoder should improve reconstruction slightly and leave FID of samples nearly unchanged. If the random-routed decoder matches it, the decoder's tokens are no more routable than diffusion tokens.
