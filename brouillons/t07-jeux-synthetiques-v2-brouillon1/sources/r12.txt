TITLE: Scale-then-frame next-scale prediction for unified image and video generation
PROPOSAL: We extend Infinity-style bitwise next-scale prediction to video by treating each frame as a sequence of scales, ordering tokens scale-major within a frame and frame-major across time, so that an image is a one-frame video and the same model handles text-to-image, text-to-video and image-to-video by changing only the conditioning prefix. We train a 2B-parameter transformer on a mix of image-text pairs and 5-second clips at 480p, then adapt it to 720p with a short second stage.
CONTROLS: Baselines: a diffusion transformer of matched size trained on the same clips, an Emu3-style token-by-token raster model, and a frame-major variant that predicts all scales of frame t only after completing frame t-1. Ablations: scale-major against frame-major ordering, bitwise against index prediction, and the image share of the data mix at two levels.
COST: 2400
PREDICTION: Scale-major ordering within a frame should reach VBench scores within a few points of the diffusion baseline at under a tenth of its latency and beat the raster model on FVD on UCF-101. If motion smoothness lags far behind the diffusion model, the within-frame scale ordering is breaking temporal coupling and the frame-major variant should then win.
=== D 2
ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Obtaining a discrete video tokenizer by temporal expansion of an image tokenizer
PROPOSAL: We take the bitwise image tokenizer of Infinity, replace each 2D convolution by a causal 3D convolution whose central temporal slice carries the image weights and whose other slices start at zero, and fine-tune only the temporal slices and the quantizer on 17-frame clips at 256p before unfreezing everything briefly at 480p. The first frame is encoded exactly as an image would be, so images and videos share one codebook and one decoder.
CONTROLS: We compare against the same architecture trained from scratch on the same clips, against a tokenizer with the image weights copied into every temporal slice, and against the plain 2D tokenizer applied frame by frame. Reconstruction is measured by PSNR, LPIPS and rFVD on UCF-101 and on a held-out web-video set, both at equal training steps and at the step where each tokenizer stops improving.
COST: 320
PREDICTION: The expanded tokenizer should reach the rFVD of the from-scratch tokenizer in a small fraction of its training steps and keep image reconstruction unchanged. If the frame-by-frame 2D tokenizer matches it on rFVD, temporal compression is not what the generator needs from the tokenizer and the gain must come from the generator side.

TITLE: Coarse-scale history for long video generation with bounded context
PROPOSAL: We give the generator a context made of the last four frames at every scale plus only the two coarsest scales of all earlier frames, so that the attended sequence grows slowly while global layout and identity remain visible. Training randomises the cut-off so the model learns to work with any history length, and we optimise for the generation of two-minute videos at 480p in rounds of eight seconds, each conditioned on a new instruction.
CONTROLS: Full-history attention up to the length that fits, a pure sliding window of four frames without any coarse history, a window that keeps only the first frame as anchor, and a key-value cache compressed by token merging at equal memory. We measure subject consistency and background consistency on VBench-Long, drift of CLIP similarity to the first frame over time, and per-frame latency as the video lengthens, over three seeds.
COST: 900
PREDICTION: Coarse-scale history should hold subject consistency within a few points of full attention while keeping per-frame latency flat, and beat the plain sliding window by a wide gap on background consistency. Failure would show up as the model ignoring the coarse history, in which case the first-frame anchor would match it.

TITLE: Text encoders and prompt adherence for single-clip discrete video generation
PROPOSAL: We train the next-scale video generator three times with different frozen text encoders, a T5-XXL encoder, a CLIP text encoder and the final layer of a decoder-only language model, on the same captioned corpus, and we measure how closely a single sixteen-frame clip follows prompts about camera motion and object actions, since adherence to instructions is what later rounds would build on.
CONTROLS: The three encoders at equal token length, a model with the text dropped at training time as the floor, a shuffled-caption check for the judge, and the adherence reported for Emu3 on the same prompts. Adherence is judged by a vision-language model and verified by two raters on 200 clips, with VBench and FVD.
COST: 1000
PREDICTION: The decoder-only encoder should give the best adherence on motion prompts and T5 the best on object prompts. If all three tie, the bottleneck is the tokenizer, not the text side.
