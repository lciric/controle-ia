TITLE: A discrete quality distribution head trained from pairwise preferences
PROPOSAL: We replace the scalar value head of a discriminative reward model with a head that outputs a categorical distribution over five quality levels, and we train it on pairwise data from Skywork-Reward-Preference and HelpSteer2 with a Bradley-Terry likelihood applied to the expected level of each response, plus an entropy term that keeps the distribution from collapsing. COST: 480
PREDICTION: We expect ranking accuracy within a point of the scalar head and better separation of good from bad responses at a fixed threshold on held-out absolute ratings. Calibration may fail on domains absent from the pairwise data, such as code.
=== D 2
ACTION: ADD
TITLE: Beta-distributed rewards that carry annotator disagreement
PROPOSAL: We train a reward head that outputs the two shape parameters of a Beta distribution over the quality of a response on the unit interval. For a pair, the training target is the probability that a draw for the chosen response exceeds a draw for the rejected one, computed in closed form, so that pairs on which annotators would disagree are fitted by overlapping distributions rather than by a large score gap. We fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B-instruct on HelpSteer2-Preference, which records several annotators per pair, and on UltraFeedback, and we test whether the predicted variance tracks the observed annotator split, whether the mean serves as an absolute score, and whether Best-of-N selection on AlpacaEval and GSM8K improves when candidates with high variance are discounted.
CONTROLS: A scalar Bradley-Terry head on the same backbone and data, the Beta head trained on majority labels with the split discarded, a head trained on labels shuffled within prompt as placebo, correlation of predicted variance with annotator split on held-out pairs, three seeds.
COST: 640
PREDICTION: We expect predicted variance to correlate clearly with annotator split and ranking accuracy to match the scalar head. Discounting high-variance candidates should help Best-of-N on chat and not on arithmetic, where disagreement is rare.

TITLE: Ordinal thresholds anchor pairwise rewards to an absolute scale
PROPOSAL: We model response quality as a latent scalar from a reward head and learn a set of ordered cut points that map the latent to five quality bins, as in cumulative-link ordinal regression. Pairwise preference pairs train the latent through a Bradley-Terry likelihood, and a small set of pointwise ratings from HelpSteer2 trains the cut points and a single affine map of the latent, so that the abundant pairwise data fixes the ranking and the scarce absolute data fixes the scale. We fine-tune Llama-3.1-8B-instruct on Skywork-Reward-Preference for the pairwise part, vary the number of pointwise ratings from a few dozen to the full set, and evaluate ranking accuracy on RewardBench, bin accuracy against held-out ratings, and the precision of a fixed acceptance threshold on chat, mathematics and code candidates.
CONTROLS: A scalar Bradley-Terry head with scores standardised per domain, the same head followed by Platt scaling on the pointwise ratings, a head trained on pointwise ratings alone, and cut points fitted to shuffled ratings as placebo, each over three seeds.
COST: 400
PREDICTION: We expect the ranking accuracy to stay unchanged while bin accuracy rises steeply with the first hundred ratings and then saturates. Threshold precision should transfer across domains better than for standardised scalar scores, which fail where the domain mean shifts.
=== D 4

TITLE: Predicting the annotator split instead of the majority label
PROPOSAL: We train a reward model to predict, for a pair of responses, the fraction of annotators who prefer the first one, using the per-pair agreement counts available in HelpSteer2-Preference and in the multi-annotator subset of PKU-SafeRLHF, with a cross-entropy loss against the soft label rather than the majority vote. COST: 560
PREDICTION: We expect soft-label training to give lower calibration error and equal ranking accuracy, and the predicted split to flag the pairs on which held-out annotators disagree. The scale fixed by soft labels may still drift across domains, which the threshold evaluation will reveal.
